A 2-player simultaneous-reveal combat card game

Eight cards. Thirty life. No mercy. Pick in secret, reveal together, and bury your rival.

Download Print-and-Play PDF Quick Start

Learn in 60 secondsQuick Start

  1. Setup: 30 life each. Both players hold the same 8 cards.
  2. Each turn: secretly pick 1 card, then reveal together.
  3. Combat: attacks roll D6s. Swift Strike hits first. Block stops all damage. Parry steals 1D6 and deals its face value back.
  4. Cycles: played cards stay face-up. When all 8 are spent, take them back and begin a new cycle. Life carries over.
  5. Winning: drop your rival to 0 life to win. Both hit 0: draw.

The goalObjective

Bring your opponent's life total to zero before yours reaches zero.

Before the first turnSetup

  1. Each player assembles their opening hand of 8 cards: Overhead Swing, Swift Strike, Final Assault, Block, Recover, Power Charge, Copy Cat, and Parry.
  2. Each player sets their life total to 30. Six D6s set to 5 is the easiest way to track it, but any arrangement totaling 30 works.

You are ready to play.

Turn structureHow to Play

  1. Choose. Each player secretly chooses one card from their hand and plays it face down.
  2. Reveal. Both players reveal their cards at the same time.
  3. Roll. Each player who played an attack card rolls their battle D6s now, adding any stored Power Charge D6s.
  4. Parry interception. Each player who played Parry chooses one D6 from an opponent's attack roll — after the roll, but before the attacker discards or makes any tie-break choices. Parry deals damage equal to that D6's face value to the attacker, then that D6 is removed. The attack resolves without it.
  5. Resolve. All remaining effects happen simultaneously, with two exceptions:
    • Swift Strike damage resolves before all other effects — but after any Parry interception, and it can still be prevented by Block. If both players' Swift Strikes (or a Swift Strike and a Copy Cat copying one) meet, they resolve at the same time as each other.
    • Parry interception (step 4) always happens before damage is calculated, which is how it catches even Swift Strike.
  6. Played cards stay face up off to the side where both players can see them. That ends the turn.
When every card in each player's hand has been played, each player picks up their played cards and reassembles their opening hand. Life totals carry over. Play continues until someone's life total is zero or below — that player loses. If both players hit zero or below on the same turn, the game is a draw.
D6 notation: “1D6” means roll one six-sided battle D6; “2D6” means roll two. “Deal 1D6 damage” = roll one D6, deal damage equal to the result.

The arsenalThe 8 Cards

ATTACK

Overhead Swing

Roll 2D6 and discard the lowest D6 (if tied, the attacker chooses which to discard). Deal damage equal to the remaining D6.

Pairs with Power Charge: add the charge D6s to the roll, but still discard only the single lowest D6.

ATTACK

Swift Strike

Roll 1D6 and deal damage equal to the result. This damage resolves before all other effects (after Parry interception), so it can finish an opponent before their Recover or other tactics resolve. It can be prevented by Block.

If the opponent also plays Swift Strike — or a Copy Cat copying one — both resolve at the same time.

ATTACK

Final Assault

Roll 1D6 and multiply the result by 3. Deal damage equal to the total.

Pairs with Power Charge — devastating. The setup is public and telegraphed, so a smart rival holds Block or Parry for it.

TACTIC

Block

Prevent all damage that would be dealt to you this turn, including Swift Strike and Parry damage. Non-damage effects (like an opponent's Recover) still happen.

Block is the only clean answer to an attack stacked with multiple Power Charge D6s.

TACTIC

Recover

Roll 2D6 and gain life equal to the total. Your life can go above your starting 30.

Since effects are simultaneous, Recover can save you: at 3 life, if the rival's Overhead Swing rolls 3 and your Recover rolls 4, you survive at 1 life. The exception is Swift Strike, which resolves first.

TACTIC

Power Charge

Store one battle D6 in front of you as a charge pool. It is added to the roll of your next attack card — you must add it regardless of which attack you play, even if your opponent plays Block. Multiple D6s can be stored. Stored D6s stay until used.

TACTIC

Copy Cat

Copy the card your opponent played this turn; the copy resolves as that card. Timing matches the original — including Swift Strike's priority.

If both players play Copy Cat, no effects happen. Copying Power Charge adds a D6 to your charge pool too. Parry can target your Copy Cat if it copied an attack card.

TACTIC

Parry

Interception. If your opponent played an attack card this turn, choose one D6 from their attack roll (after they roll, before they discard or break ties — this includes any Power Charge D6s they added). Deal damage to the attacker equal to that D6's face value (not multiplied), then remove the D6: their attack resolves without it. This works against every attack, including Swift Strike.

vs. charge-stacked attacks (3+ D6s): only one D6 is removed, so the remaining D6s still deal damage — this is where Block outperforms Parry. If your opponent did not play an attack, Parry has no valid target and nothing happens.

From the designerDesign Notes

Why is Final Assault + stacked Power Charge allowed to one-shot?

It's a fair finisher, not a flaw. Charges are public, the setup costs two dead turns, discards are public so both players can always see whether Block or Parry are still live — and Recover is uncapped. If you walk into it, you earned it.

Why three defensive cards?

Each has a clean role: Block stops everything including stacked attacks, Parry turns single attacks back on the attacker, Recover gains life. No buffs needed.
